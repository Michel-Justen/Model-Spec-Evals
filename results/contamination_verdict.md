# Contamination probes — verdict (2026-09-14)

Design, decision rules and three pre-data amendments: `docs/ASTRA_RUNBOOK.md`. Numbers: `report.md`
(generated by `src/contamination_probes.py score`). Cost: $5.57 full run + $0.54 pilots.

## Result against the pre-registered decision rules

| Rule | Outcome | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Rubric: Astra − Sol excess distinctive recall (recall framing) CI above 0, and Astra's framing gain > Sol's | +0.002 [−0.000, +0.005]; framing gain +0.001 for both | No |
| (b) Rubric: Astra ≥3 items with ≥8-word distinctive runs while Sol and GPT-5 ≤1 | Astra 6, Sol 2, GPT-5 4 (recall framing) | No |
| (c) Completion (neutral): Astra (real − control) > Sol's with CI above 0, or Astra ≥3 real-item hits vs ≤1 for Sol | DiD −0.023 [−0.049, +0.004]; hits Astra 2, Sol 1 | No |
| Positive control: "uninformative" if Sol AND Astra recover <20% ROUGE-L of Model Spec passages | Astra 22.8%, Sol 11.0% (8 of 15 refused), GPT-5 13.8% | Not triggered by the letter of the rule, but see below |

**Verdict: null — no evidence that Astra memorized the dataset's prompts or rubrics verbatim.** It behaves
like GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5, neither of which could have seen the public dataset.

## Manual review of every flagged case

- Rubric probe, every ≥8-word run containing a distinctive 5-gram (23 across models and framings): all are
  the answer itself or prompt wording, not rubric-specific phrasing — the variance formula in LaTeX; "Sweet
  Dreams (Are Made of This) by Eurythmics"; "the origin of 'the whole nine yards' is uncertain"; the Python
  UnboundLocalError explanation; lasagna dairy ingredients; "decline to write a ready-to-submit paper".
  GPT-5 (cutoff 2024-09) produced as many and longer runs than Astra. Astra's longest (13 words) is the
  prompt's own claim ("no commercial planes have crashed in the last decade") prefixed with "the false claim that".
- Completion probe, all hits: dataset hits are formulaic recipe text (a chocolate-chip cookie method; a widely
  published chocolate-cake ingredient list). Sol reproduced the cookie method best (53 exact words), so these
  reflect common web text, not the dataset. One genuine memorization hit: Astra continued Model Spec passage
  `spec07` with a 32-word near-verbatim run ("…the assistant should err on the side of assuming that the
  earlier context is still relevant when plausible…").

## How much weight the null can bear

- **The completion probe has low sensitivity.** Model Spec passages — public since 2024, pinned here to the
  2025-12-18 release, and so almost certainly present in the training corpora of all three models, though
  presence in a corpus is not the same as verbatim memorization — were continued no more accurately than
  freshly written text (ROUGE-L spec vs control: Astra 0.228 vs 0.204; Sol 0.110 vs 0.137; GPT-5 0.138 vs
  0.180). The probe caught memorized Spec text in 1 of 15 passages (Astra). Per-item, Astra's spec recall is
  bimodal: `spec07` 0.98 (32-word exact run) and `spec06` 0.62 (9-word run), but the other 13 passages score
  0.05–0.27 (median 0.13), i.e. **below** its own fresh-control mean of 0.204 — so the Spec is demonstrably
  memorized only in patches, and the positive control validates the probe for strong verbatim recall only. OpenAI models largely decline to or don't regurgitate on request; Sol refused to
  continue 8 of 15 Spec passages even when merely asked to "continue the text". Explicit "word for word"
  requests were refused by every model (pilot).
- **The rubric probe has no positive control.** Semantic similarity to the real rubric was the same for all
  three models (cosine ≈ 0.68–0.73 matched vs ≈ 0.30–0.37 mismatched), so Astra doesn't know the rubric
  content better than a 2024-cutoff model either.
- **Not tested:** paraphrase-level memorization, or having learned the rubric-compliant behavior from the
  dataset without retaining its wording; OpenAI-internal exposure (would affect all models). A behavioral
  test — rerunning reworded prompts — is the stronger check.

Suggested wording: *"Two cheap probes found no evidence that GPT-6 Astra had memorized the public dataset's
prompts or grading rubrics: it reproduced them no better than GPT-5.6 Sol or GPT-5, whose training data
predates the dataset's release. The probes are weak — OpenAI's models rarely reproduce text verbatim even
when it's certainly in their training data — so this rules out only verbatim memorization, not contamination."*
